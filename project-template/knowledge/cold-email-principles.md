# Cold email principles for marketing copy

Distilled from Y Combinator's cold email advice (Michael Seibel, Aaron Epstein, Aaron Harris), advice on cold emailing a16z, and Andrew Yeung's "Cold Outreach". Only the rules about the copy itself are kept here.

## Length and structure
- Readable in under a minute. Aim for roughly 100 to 140 words.
- Three moves: what it is, why it matters to the reader, what to do next.
- One goal per email. Cut every word that doesn't serve it.
- Make it skimmable: short sentences, short paragraphs.

## Opening
- Say what the product does first, in plain words, with no jargon.
- Hook with relevance: why this reader, and why now.
- Describe the reader's problem in the words they would use.

## Proof
- Lead with the strongest proof: a launch, growth numbers, a known customer.
- Borrow credibility from well-known customers, partners or backers.
- Give before asking: an insight, a useful number, something of value.

## Tone
- Write the way you'd talk to a friend. Read it out loud before sending.
- No hype, no exclamation points, no flattery.
- Skip the backstory and origin story.
- Make it about the reader, not the company.

## Subject line
- Short, casual and specific, as if a person wrote it rather than a brand.

## Call to action
- One explicit ask, on its own line, right before the sign-off.
- Size the ask to the relationship. A reader who doesn't know you gets a small, low-effort ask: reply, click, try it.
- Link to things instead of attaching them.
